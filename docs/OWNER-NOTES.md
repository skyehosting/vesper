# Notes for you — what you asked for, what Vesper does, and why

| You asked for | Vesper does | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Memory "stored within Voyage" | Your whole timeline — every message with its role tag ("user response" / "ai response"), your device's local date and time, and the chat ID — plus the list of your chats and the search index live **on this PC** in `%APPDATA%\Vesper`. Voyage is only asked to turn text into searchable numbers (and to rank search results), and only while "Remember with Voyage AI" is on. | Voyage AI is an embedding/ranking service; it has no database to keep your conversations in. Keeping memory locally is also far better for privacy and works offline (keyword search still works with no internet). |
| The "$5 to stop Voyage training on your data" | Corrected in the app's privacy texts: by default Voyage may use what you send to train its models. To opt out you need a **payment method on file** and must **switch off data use yourself** as an admin of your Voyage organization. There's no $5 minimum — the only "$5" in their terms is a liability cap. Paying alone doesn't opt you out; opting out only covers data sent afterwards, Voyage may cancel your free tokens when you opt out, and undoing it means emailing Voyage. | Verified against Voyage's terms, FAQ and MongoDB's docs on 2026-10-05 (research 02, independently re-checked). |
| `[tone=…]` at the **end** of the AI's reply — and, after using 1.0.0, a switch for tones that follow the conversation instead of changing with every reply | Settings → Voice out → "Voice tones", right under the voice (also on the setup wizard's voice step, and `/voice tone off`, `/voice tone conversation` or `/voice tone reply` in a chat): **"Follow the conversation"** (the default) — the AI sets a tone in its first spoken reply and writes a new one only when the mood of the conversation changes; replies in between keep the current tone, also after Vesper restarts. **"Every reply"** — a fresh tone for every spoken reply (how 1.0.0 worked). **"Off"** — one even tone; the AI isn't asked for tags and any it writes anyway are removed, never shown or spoken. Only voices that can change their tone get one: ElevenLabs, OpenAI's gpt-4o-mini-tts, an OpenAI-compatible server running a gpt-4o model, and the Windows voices (lightly: a little faster or slower, higher or lower). With any other voice the page says so and the AI isn't asked to write tags. A change in the middle of a chat reaches the AI as a short note from then on. The tag goes at the **start** of the reply by default ("Where the AI puts the tone" lets you choose "At the end"). | A new tone on every reply made the voice change mood from one answer to the next even when the conversation hadn't — it sounded like a tone per response rather than one that follows how the conversation is going. Following the conversation sounds like one person whose mood shifts when the talk does, and it saves the AI writing a tag on most replies. The voice can only start speaking in the right tone if it knows the tone before the first sentence. With "At the end", Vesper would have to wait for the whole reply before speaking (short replies) or speak the beginning in the previous tone (long ones). |
| Bracket functions like `[memory_search]` in a protocols file | The protocols file (Settings → Memory → Protocols) describes the functions, and models that support it get them as native "tools" with the same names and parameters; other models use the bracket form. | Native tool calls are more reliable for big cloud models; brackets work everywhere. Both are generated from one definition, so they never disagree. |
| Windows text-to-speech | Uses the Windows voices available to apps (David, Zira, Mark on your PC), fully offline. | Windows 11's newer "natural" Narrator voices aren't available to other apps. ElevenLabs or OpenAI voices sound far better if you have a key. |
| Access from outside your network | Settings → Access & security → "Anywhere, with Tailscale" — a free, private network between your own devices; phones get a trusted HTTPS address, so the microphone works and Vesper can be added to the home screen. Port forwarding and tunnels (Cloudflare Tunnel, ngrok and the like) aren't supported: Vesper only answers at the addresses it sets up itself. | Exposing a chat app that holds your API keys directly to the internet is risky, and tunnel services decrypt your traffic on their own servers. Tailscale keeps it private and encrypted end to end, with no router changes. |
| Load 100 messages at a time, unload below | Pages of 100 (20–300 in Settings → Chat); about 3 pages stay in view and the rest unload as you scroll, so a million messages stay smooth. A timeline scrubber jumps anywhere. | Browsers can't lay out a million messages at once (Chromium caps page height around 15 million pixels). |
| Very short messages in memory | Messages under 3 words aren't sent to Voyage (they're still found by keyword search). | They don't help search by meaning and cost requests on the free Voyage tier. |
| Text fades in with the voice | Text is held until its audio is ready and then revealed letter by letter in time with the voice. Code blocks, tables and maths aren't spoken (Vesper can say "code block" instead: Settings → Voice out → Advanced); they appear whole when the voice reaches them. | Reading code aloud isn't useful; the reveal still finishes exactly when the audio ends. |
| After 1.0.0: the AI in the centre of the chat area behind the text, not in its own bar, as a fluid sphere with a line that is the audio visualizer — then, for Armilla, only the main ring showing the audio wave and no wobble on the inner rings | **Armilla** is the new default (Settings → Presence & appearance → "Star style"; orb, nebula, Minimal and Off stay). It sits centred behind the messages and never takes a click, a scroll or a selection. The long horizon line is the only part that moves with the voice; the inner rings keep turning (slowly against each other while Vesper speaks) but never move, brighten or flicker with it — measured on the real GPU, a quiet and a loud frame differ by at most 5 of 255 levels over 7,598 ring pixels, and that little is the bead's glow lying over them. An empty chat shows it large; in a conversation it is **dimmed under the text** and comes forward a little while Vesper speaks; Talk mode shows it full size; phones draw it in 2D. If your settings came from 1.0.0 with the 1.0 default style (the orb), Vesper switches them to Armilla once; choose the orb again and it stays. | Text drawn over a bright moving picture gets hard to read: behind the messages the avatar's brightness is capped so body text keeps at least 4.5:1 contrast on its brightest frame (measured on the real GPU), and it is at full strength only where no text is drawn over it. 1.0.0 saved every setting, defaults included, so without the one-time switch an updated install would keep the orb. |

## Things only your keys can confirm
Vesper's tests use mock services, so a few behaviours were verified against documentation but not live:
- ElevenLabs v4: that `/with-timestamps` returns reliable character timing (Vesper validates every response and falls back to its own timing estimate if not).
- Voyage free-trial limits (3 requests/minute, 10K tokens/minute) — Vesper starts in that mode and speeds up automatically.
- Speech recognition with your real microphone, and echo cancellation when the AI speaks through speakers. Interrupting the voice is "Tap or type" by default; choose "Just start talking" in Settings → Voice in after the echo test, or with headphones.
- Anthropic's newest models with a long conversation (the history is replayed byte-for-byte as Anthropic requires).

From the source folder after `npm install` (the installed app has no npm), run `npm run live-check` with your keys in environment variables (the user guide's "Checking with your own keys", docs/GUIDE.md, lists their names) to check these in a minute. It makes a few small requests per service (listing models is free; a few cents at most in all), prints a PASS / WARN / FAIL report, and never saves or prints a key. With no key set it calls nothing.
